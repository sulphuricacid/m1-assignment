Happy Tails: Recent Success Stories

Nothing keeps our volunteers going like an update from an adopted pet's new family. Here are a few of our favorite recent "happy tails" from around Georgia.

Max the Golden — Marietta

Max spent eight months in a shelter after his previous owner moved away. The Johnson family met him at our Piedmont Park adoption event, and it was love at first fetch.

"We thought we were rescuing Max, but honestly, he rescued us. Our kids haven't touched a screen on a sunny Saturday since." — The Johnson family

Luna the Midnight Kitten — Savannah

Black cats are statistically among the last to be adopted from shelters — a phenomenon shelters call "black cat syndrome." Luna waited five months before a college student took a chance on her.

"She sits on my desk during every online lecture. My study buddy has a 4.0 GPA in napping." — Priya, Savannah

Scout the Senior Beagle — Decatur

Remember Scout from our adoptable pets page? A retired teacher adopted him last spring, proving that senior pets make the sweetest companions.

"At our age, neither of us runs marathons. We nap, we stroll, we share biscuits. It's perfect." — Ms. Eleanor, Decatur

Fun Facts to Impress Your Friends

We researched these on Wikipedia so you don't have to. Warning: may cause spontaneous pet adoption.

  • A dog's sense of smell is estimated to be tens of thousands of times more sensitive than ours — Max can smell what you had for breakfast yesterday.
  • Cats spend up to 70% of their lives sleeping — around 13 to 16 hours a day. Luna's 4.0 in napping is real.
  • A cat's purr vibrates at frequencies that some studies suggest may promote healing — a built-in wellness machine.
  • Rabbits do a joyful twisting leap called a binky when they're happy. Yes, it's as adorable as it sounds.
  • Dogs have been our companions for a very long time — domestication began at least 14,000 years ago, before agriculture existed.
  • A group of kittens is called a kindle, and a group of pups is a litter — now you can win trivia night.

Space required Depends on breed; a yard is a big plus Happy in apartments Needs a roomy indoor enclosure plus play space

Typical lifespan 10–13 years 12–18 years 8–12 years
Best for Active families and outdoor lovers Busy professionals and quiet homes Gentle, patient owners with calm households
